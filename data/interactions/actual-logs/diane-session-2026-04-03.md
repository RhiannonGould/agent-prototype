# Diane Kowalski — Session Log
**Date:** 2026-04-03  
**Skills Used:** session-start, want-examination, reframe (boundary-setting)  
**Persona AI Usage Pattern:** Avoidance  

---

## Opening Statement
**Diane:** "I am Diane, I am thinking about using AI for simple tasks but I fear that this will be ethically incorrect and alter who I am as a person."

## Conversation Flow

### Phase 1: Usage Examination (want-examination)

**Agent:** Asked Diane to walk through what "altering who she is" looks like in her mind.

**Diane:** She doesn't want to lose her writing skills and has ethical concerns about AI's impact on the field of writing.

**Agent:** Asked about a recent moment where the fear of losing skills felt real.

**Diane:** She struggles to reword sentences professionally without taking a lot of time. She would like editor support, but all available editors use built-in generative AI.

**Agent:** Asked what the editing process feels like — draining or still feels like hers.

**Diane:** It is draining.

**Agent:** Suggested the exhaustion of protecting her craft might be wearing down her relationship with writing.

**Diane:** That feels off.

**Agent:** Asked what the exhaustion is actually coming from.

**Diane:** She is trying to keep up with other writers who are using AI.

**Agent:** Named the dynamic — running a race where everyone else has a bicycle. Asked what the speed gap feels like.

**Diane:** They feel faster and it feels unfair.

**Agent:** Asked whether the unfairness is about freelance deadlines or something deeper.

**Diane:** It's about what her expertise is worth now.

**Agent:** Asked whether the people closest to her work — students, editors, readers — value her differently, or if it's the industry that shifted.

**Diane:** The industry has shifted around them.

**Agent:** So the people who matter still see her value. Asked whether using AI for something small (like finding a word) would feel like giving into the system.

**Diane:** Yes, it feels like giving into the system.

**Agent:** Reflected that her position is principled, not reactive — it costs her something and she holds it anyway. Asked if there's any version of tool use that wouldn't feel like giving in.

**Diane:** She isn't aware of her options for tools.

**Agent:** Asked whether she's open to exploring options or just noting the information gap.

**Diane:** She is open to exploring.

**Agent:** Asked what part of her writing process she would never want a tool to touch.

**Diane:** She only wants a tool that edits her work. She does NOT ever want it to generate full sentences or ideas.

### Phase 2: Boundary Setting (reframe)

**Agent:** Validated the distinction between generating and refining. Proposed designing a small, bounded experiment.

**Diane:** Agreed to try.

**Agent:** Asked what piece of writing to try it with — low-stakes or the freelance article.

**Diane:** Something low-stakes.

**Agent:** Asked what low-stakes writing comes to mind.

**Diane:** A discussion post for classwork.

**Agent:** Asked how long the experiment should run.

**Diane:** Two tries.

**Agent:** Asked what would be okay for the tool to do, and what would cross the line.

**Diane:**
- **Okay:** Suggesting a single word change, minimal restructuring (if she can compare original to new), catching grammatical errors.
- **Crosses the line:** Completely rewriting phrases, sentences, or paragraphs.

**Agent:** Asked what moment during the experiment might make her uncomfortable.

**Diane:** If she started using the AI to generate original drafts or add completely new sentences.

**Agent:** Asked Diane to describe the experiment in her own words.

**Diane:** "Use two discussion posts for editing with AI and I will stop once it crosses my boundary."

**Agent:** Suggested she notice how she *felt* after each try — like having an editor, or like giving something away. Offered a closing reflection.

**Diane:** Declined the reflection.

**Agent:** Respected the choice. Closed the session.

---

## Experiment Designed

| Component | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Scope** | Editing-only AI tool on discussion posts. No generation of ideas, sentences, or original content. |
| **Duration** | Two tries |
| **Acceptable use** | Word suggestions, minimal restructuring (with before/after comparison), grammar corrections |
| **Boundary** | No rewriting of phrases, sentences, or paragraphs. No generating original drafts or new sentences. |
| **Friction signal** | Temptation to ask the tool to write something new = close the tool immediately |
| **Success metric** | How she *feels* using it — like having an editor, or like giving something away |

## Key Insights
- Diane's resistance to AI is principled and consistent, not reactive or fearful.
- The core pain is about the devaluation of her expertise by industry shifts, not about the tools themselves.
- The people closest to her work still value what she brings — the pressure is systemic, not personal.
- She drew a very clear line: editing yes, generating no. This distinction is central to her identity as a writer.
- Exhaustion comes from competing on speed with AI-assisted writers, not from the writing process itself.
